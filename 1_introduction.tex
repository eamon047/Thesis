\section{Introduction}
\label{chap:introduction}

Knowledge graphs organize information as entities and relations and provide a
structured representation of facts. They are used in applications such as
semantic search, recommendation, and question answering. More recently,
knowledge graphs have also been combined with large language models in
retrieval-augmented generation, where structured knowledge can be retrieved as
external information during inference \citep{pan2024roadmap}. However,
real-world knowledge graphs are often incomplete, and some valid links between
entities are missing. Link prediction aims to recover these missing links
\citep{ji2022survey}.

Knowledge graph embedding (KGE) models perform link prediction by learning
vector representations of entities and relations. For a query with a missing
head or tail entity, a KGE model assigns scores to candidate entities and ranks
them according to these scores. Model performance is usually evaluated using
aggregate ranking metrics. Hits@10, for example, records whether the correct
entity is ranked among the top ten candidates and averages this outcome over
the test queries. This metric summarizes overall accuracy, but it does not show
whether two models with similar scores succeed on the same queries. Two models
can therefore obtain similar Hits@10 values while making different top-10
decisions for individual queries.

This type of query-level instability is referred to as predictive
multiplicity. Previous work introduced predictive multiplicity for KGE-based
link prediction and showed that models with comparable overall performance can
produce different predictions for the same query
\citep{zhu2024predictive}. That study summarized multiplicity mainly at the
global model level. This leaves open whether the instability is distributed
evenly across relations or concentrated in particular relation patterns.

This thesis examines predictive multiplicity at the relation level using
FB15k-237 and repeated runs of RotatE and TransE. We compare relation-level
Hits@10 and prediction disagreement across repeated runs for three relation
patterns: how many entities a relation connects on each side (mapping type),
whether another relation connects the same entities in reverse order (inverse),
and whether the relation itself connects entity pairs in both directions
(symmetry). The results show that predictive multiplicity is not evenly
distributed across relations. The clearest differences appear for mapping type
and depend on which side is predicted. A small group of relations with clear
inverse partners has lower multiplicity, while symmetry shows no clear
association. This relation-level view complements aggregate accuracy by showing
where prediction instability is concentrated. It may also help future work
focus on the relations and prediction directions with greater instability.

The remainder of this thesis is organized as follows. Chapter 2 introduces
knowledge graphs, KGE models, link prediction evaluation, and the relation
patterns considered in this thesis. Chapter 3 reviews model multiplicity in
machine learning and previous work on predictive multiplicity in KGE-based
link prediction. Chapter 4 defines the relation-level analysis framework and
the metrics used in this thesis. Chapter 5 presents the experimental setup and
results. Finally, Chapter 6 summarizes the findings, discusses the limitations
of the current analysis, and outlines directions for future work.
